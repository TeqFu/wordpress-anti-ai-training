# WordPress Anti-AI Training

Until policy-makers and congressmen can figure out that AI training on other people's content without compensation while selling it back to people through subscriptions (and even free plans), is nothing short of piracy, we must take it upon ourselves to protect our intellectual property. 

## Because Human Creativity Matters and Should have Fair Compensation

- Imagine going to work and not getting a paycheck that week. You worked without compensation.
- You learn a trade working for company x and the day you want a salary increase, they want you to train the new hire.

The technology of AI is an amazing tool on its own merit. However, the way it's being deployed levels the uniqueness of every creator, pulling their talents away and spoon-feeding it to those who lack. Ironically, it seems to parallel with Robin Hood; taking from the rich and giving to the poor. It seems noble if you don't think about it, though it's piracy no matter, especially if the rich & talented worked hard to get where they are. If we were to envision an ethical AI ecosystem, we'd want creators from which AI scrapes data to be compensated in some fashion - either through:

- A commission on monthly subscriptions based on the percentage of a creator's influence in the AI output,
- A flat rate upfront compensation to scrape upon consented agreement,
- Or in consented marketing exposure through providing links back to the source of the training data.
- or provide donation links to the creators on every AI output.

If I read a book that's within copyrights without paying for it, and make a profit from the information, is that considered piracy? If I purchased the book from the author, then the answer is no, because I purchased it. Otherwise, it borders on piracy. The human mind's limitation to storing and recalling knowledge has somewhat been the limiting factor to piracy until AI blasted through those limitations; scraping every site and resource it has internet access to.

## Protecting our Intellectual Property (IP)

Since ~40% of the Internet is comprised of WordPress and we also build sites on that platform, we'd like to share small developments, scripts, plugins, and various other little tid-bits that can reduce IP piracy.
